## Network
Times Square stands as one of the densest retail and commercial hubs in New York, where street networks and storefronts intersect. By mapping retail shops across this pedestrian grid, the analysis reveals how commercial storefronts cluster directly along major walking paths and street intersections. This urban network perspective shows how retail density is structured by the underlying street geometry, driving foot traffic and shaping the commercial activity of the area.

To fully examine this urban fabric, the study unfolds across four key sequential areas:

Mapping the foundational pedestrian walking network, capturing its structural edges and intersection nodes as the basic spatial framework.

Shortest-Path Routing: Simulating movement across the grid with shortest-path algorithms to trace explicit directional trajectories through the dense blocks.

Retail Shop Distribution: Extracting and plotting real-time commercial storefront locations to identify patterns of retail concentration.

Overlaying the network, retail points, and routing paths onto a dark-mode canvas to visualize how spatial connectivity and commercial density dynamically interact.
