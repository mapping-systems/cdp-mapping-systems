01 · Premise

Public access is promised uniformly. Public benefit is not.

Privately owned public spaces—or POPS—are provided and maintained by private property owners in exchange for zoning benefits.

If these outdoor plazas share an obligation to provide public access, how consistent is the breadth of amenities they are required to provide?

Source: NYC Department of City Planning, Privately Owned Public Spaces

02 · Study area

One corridor makes unequal requirements easier to see.

The study follows Sixth Avenue from approximately West 42nd to West 57th Street. Holding the corridor relatively constant focuses the comparison on documented requirements rather than broad neighborhood differences.

    06 · Contrasts

    Comparable access requirements can produce very different records.

    07 · Limits

    The database maps obligation, not experience.

    The official record does not confirm whether amenities are currently present, maintained, welcoming, or equally accessible. Mixed developments may aggregate several public-space components.

    Looking forward

    The next step is verification.

    A future version would compare legal records against current imagery, accessibility audits, and field observations—shifting the question from what is promised to what the public can actually use.
