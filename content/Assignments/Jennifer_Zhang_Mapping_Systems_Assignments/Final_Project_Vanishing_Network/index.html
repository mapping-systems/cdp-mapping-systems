Method / sources

Real ground. Bounded claims.

What is spatially real

The land surface streams a digital elevation model. The county boundary, 385 settlement points, arterial roads, rail geometry, four station points and two school points are current public-map records clipped to Hailun.

How the 3D bars are built

Each column aggregates a 3 by 3 group of WorldPop 1 km population cells. Boundary cells are fractionally clipped to Hailun, and each 2010 and 2020 grid is normalized so all bars sum to that year's official census population. Height is linear: one resident equals 0.18 map meters. In 2020, the pale lower volume is the remaining population; the coral volume between the 2020 and 2010 heights is gross cell-level population loss. Small cell-level gains are excluded from that loss volume and reported separately from the net decline.

What is measured through time

Population values are census benchmarks for 2000, 2010 and 2020. The Population evidence control also compares countywide age structure and average family-household size in 2010 and 2020. Only documented census years appear. The 2010 age shares and household size are derived from the seventh-census bulletin's published percentage-point changes. These countywide values appear as 3D chart columns anchored near Hailun: they are map-based diagrams, not estimates for the ground beneath them. The school chapter compares the compiled ordinary-primary-school counts for 2000 (390), 2010 (218) and 2014 (32). The 2022 value of 34 is derived from the official report's 26 compliant primary schools at a 76.5% rate, so the interface marks 2022 as a definition break rather than pretending the series is perfectly identical. The official unique compulsory-school total is 54. The 2D map removes the blue road network and ordinary settlement dots. Historical years render each documented county count as a schematic field distributed across mapped settlements; those comparison dots are not archived school addresses. In 2022 the schematic field disappears and exactly two current school sites with open coordinates appear. The outflow chapter moves to a national province-to-province view and compares census-defined rural-to-urban flows in 2010 and 2020; it does not infer Hailun destinations from the county's population decline. The road-grid chapter measures boundary-clipped OpenStreetMap road length in 2014, 2015, 2020 and 2025 using the ohsome history API.

How the 2D census view works

The Population chapter can lock the map to a north-up, zero-pitch view and display Hailun's 2000, 2010 and 2020 census records as countywide charts. Use the year buttons, timeline or play control to compare them. The 2000 view omits age structure because no comparable local age table was available; the 2010 age shares are reconstructed from changes published in the seventh-census bulletin. The colored 3 km cells use the matching WorldPop year normalized to each official census total. They provide spatial context but are not township-level census observations.

How interaction works

The evidence timeline is a continuous drag control that snaps to documented observations on release. Chart dots identify evidence positions but are not navigation buttons. Orbit mode makes a normal map drag rotate and tilt the terrain; switch it off to pan instead.

Road development observation

Four OpenStreetMap history snapshots show Hailun's mapped drivable-road network rising fastest between 2015 and 2020, then continuing to expand at a slower pace through 2025. The road lines become brighter and wider as the recorded network length increases.

What the migration graphic means

The arrow field borrows the frequency principle of The New York Times county movement map: more moving marks mean more people. The China view is reconstructed from the sixth- and seventh-census long-form tables. A rural origin is a hukou registration place recorded as a township or a town village committee; an urban destination is a current residence recorded as a city or town. The published 10% long-form counts are scaled by ten, following the census research convention.

Public data still do not report a Hailun-to-destination-city matrix. The national view therefore changes scale instead of inventing local routes. Every drawn direction is a real province-to-province OD pair, but the line ends at a province display anchor because the census table does not name a city. Only routes of at least 500,000 people are drawn: 23 routes in 2010 and 31 in 2020. The headline totals include all 930 possible cross-province pairs, including the smaller routes that are not drawn.

What is intentionally absent

No village-level closure dates are fabricated. The School chapter's historical dots are explicitly labeled as a schematic comparison field based on mapped settlements, not real former school addresses. Only the two 2022 points claim current open-map coordinates. Age and household evidence is countywide, so those views replace the spatial population columns with explicitly labeled countywide chart columns rather than inventing a village distribution.
